Privacy Policy

Last updated 17 August 2026. Applies to the Dutch iPhone app and this website.

Dutch collects no personal data. The app has no server, no user accounts and no analytics. Nothing you enter is sent to the developer, and there is no system anywhere that could receive it.

Where your data lives

Your groups, the people in them and every expense are stored on your device and, if you are signed in to iCloud, mirrored to your own private iCloud database using Apple's CloudKit. That storage belongs to your Apple Account. The developer has no access to it and no ability to read, export or delete it.

When you share a group, Dutch uses Apple's own CloudKit sharing — the same mechanism behind a shared photo album or a shared Note. The QR code and link are produced by Apple and grant access to that group's records only. People you share with can see and edit that group. They cannot see any of your other groups.

Some preferences are kept on your device alone and are never synced: which member of a group is you, who usually pays, the last currency and exchange rate you entered, and which group you opened most recently. These exist to save you typing.

What the app is allowed to do, and why

  • Camera — only when you open the scanner, and only to read a QR code. Nothing is recorded, stored or transmitted. Frames are processed on your device and discarded.
  • Location — off until you turn it on, and read only at the moment you tap Nearby while entering an expense. Dutch takes a single reading, asks Apple Maps which cafés and restaurants are within a short walk — or, when it finds none, what street that reading is on — and forgets it. There is no background location, no tracking and no history. Nothing about where you were is saved unless you pick one of those places: the name you picked, and the point it stands on, are then stored on that expense and sync to the group with it, like its title — so anyone in the group can open it on a map. That is the whole of what is kept, it is kept only for the expense you attached it to, and you can remove it from that expense at any time.
  • iCloud — to store your groups in your private database and to sync them between your devices and the people you share with.
  • Notifications — Dutch registers for silent push so that changes made by other people arrive on your device. Those pushes are delivered by Apple and carry no message or content; they only tell the app that something changed.

    If you switch notifications on in the app's settings, Dutch then writes the alert you see on your own device, from the expense it has just downloaded — there is no server of ours composing or sending it, and the wording never leaves your phone. Notifications are off until you turn them on, and turning them off stops them entirely.
  • Spotlight — your group names are indexed on your device so you can search for them. That index is local and is not readable by other apps.

Purchases

Dutch Unlimited is a one-time in-app purchase handled entirely by Apple. The developer never sees your payment details, card number or billing address. The app asks Apple whether this Apple Account owns the purchase and does nothing else with that answer. The purchase is recorded on your device and by Apple, not on any server run by the developer.

What the developer can see

Only what Apple reports to any developer with an app on the App Store: the number of downloads, purchases, crashes and ratings, in aggregate. This comes from Apple, not from the app, and does not identify you. If you have turned off Share With App Developers in your iOS analytics settings, Apple does not include your device at all.

No third parties

Dutch contains no advertising, no analytics SDK, no crash reporter, no tracking pixel and no third-party code of any kind. It is built with Apple's frameworks only. You can verify this — the entire source is published at github.com/lakafior/Dutch under the Mozilla Public License 2.0.

This website

This site is static HTML hosted on Cloudflare Pages. It sets no cookies, loads no fonts or scripts from other domains, and runs no JavaScript. Cloudflare processes requests to serve the page and may keep short-lived operational logs, as any web host does.

Deleting your data

Delete a group in the app and it is removed from your device and your iCloud. Delete the app and its local data goes with it; to remove what is stored in iCloud, use Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage on your device. Because the developer holds nothing, there is no request to send and nobody to ask.

Children

Dutch is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the revised version will be published here with a new date. Because the app has no data collection to expand, any change is likely to be a clarification.
